# Frontend Inspiration Research

> Goal: build a personal site that feels like a lived-in place rather than a developer template.

## References reviewed

### Maggie Appleton — https://maggieappleton.com/
What works:
- Content is treated as a garden with multiple maturity levels, not only polished posts.
- Essays, notes, patterns, library, and now-page coexist naturally.
- Illustrations and typography create an unmistakable author identity.
- The homepage explains what each content type *means* rather than just listing categories.

Borrow:
- different content maturity / granularity;
- a real `Now` page;
- content itself is the visual material.

Do not copy:
- her illustration language, card composition, or exact garden metaphor.

### Steph Ango — https://stephango.com/
What works:
- extremely quiet interface; writing remains the protagonist;
- compact top navigation and very low chrome;
- dates form an archive rhythm rather than metadata clutter;
- topics are discoverable without dominating the page.

Borrow:
- restraint;
- strong date rhythm;
- short navigation;
- writing-first information density.

Do not copy:
- exact typography or plain list layout.

### Robin Sloan — https://www.robinsloan.com/
What works:
- reads like a personal publication rather than a portfolio;
- typography feels authored and editorial;
- many different projects can coexist without turning into a résumé grid;
- small oddities and custom details make the site feel handmade.

Borrow:
- editorial voice;
- permission for the website to contain playful, non-utilitarian details;
- projects as stories, not tech-stack cards.

### pseudoyu — https://www.pseudoyu.com/
What works:
- technical work, daily life, media, photography and relationships share one identity;
- personal interests are not hidden behind a professional persona.

Borrow:
- life and engineering should coexist as equal parts of the site.

### Yunyoujun — https://yunyoujun.cn/
What works:
- strong sense of a personal internet home;
- diary-like writing can sit next to projects and technical content;
- the site allows personality and small experiments.

Borrow:
- make room for ordinary life and small playful pages.

## Design conclusion

The site should not be a conventional blog template and should not be a portfolio dashboard.

It should feel like:

**a field journal + personal publication + living archive.**

Primary visual job: make time, writing, photographs, and unfinished thoughts feel like parts of one life.

## Anti-patterns for this project

- no giant centered SaaS hero;
- no GitHub stats wall;
- no identical rounded cards for every content type;
- no purple/blue gradient identity;
- no terminal/dev aesthetic as the whole brand;
- no excessive tags, categories, sidebars, counters;
- no decorative motion on every section;
- no copy such as “Welcome to my blog” or “Explore my journey”.

## Signature-element candidates

1. **Time seam** — a quiet vertical thread that connects dated fragments on the homepage and Moments page.
2. **Margin notes** — short handwritten/editorial side notes that make selected pages feel annotated rather than templated.
3. **Monthly folio** — each month can have a subtle issue/folio identity, making the archive feel like volumes of a life.

The three prototypes intentionally test these ideas separately before committing to one design system.
